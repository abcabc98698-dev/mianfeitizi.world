Walking into a yoga studio for the first time can feel intimidating. Everyone else seems to know where to put their mat, what the props are for and when to bow their head. The good news: almost everyone in the room once felt exactly the same way.

Here is what you can expect, step by step.

## Before you arrive

Choose a session labelled *beginner* or *all levels* from our [studio practice sessions](/#sessions). Our Hatha Foundations and Yin & Restore sessions are both ideal starting points because they move at an unhurried pace and explain each posture clearly.

Wear clothes you can move and stretch in. Leggings or shorts with a fitted top work well, as loose shirts tend to slide forward in inversions and folds. Bring a water bottle. We provide mats, blocks, straps, bolsters and fresh towels at no extra charge.

## When you get to the studio

Aim to arrive ten minutes before your [scheduled class time](/#timetable). That gives you plenty of room to:

1. Check in at the reception desk and complete a brief wellness note.
2. Find a spot and unroll your mat. The back row is always welcome — you will still clearly see and hear your instructor.
3. Introduce yourself to your instructor and mention any injuries, pregnancy or areas you would like to support gently.

## During the session

Most foundational sessions follow a steady arc:

- **Arriving:** a few quiet minutes seated or supine while you [settle your breath](/journal/daily-pranayama-breathwork-techniques-for-calm/).
- **Warming up:** gentle mobilizations for the spine, hips and shoulders.
- **Main sequence:** standing and seated postures, with clear variations to make each shape more accessible or more challenging.
- **Cooling down:** slower floor stretches to release residual tension.
- **Final rest:** several quiet minutes lying down in Savasana at the close.

You will hear traditional posture names alongside plain English cues. Do not worry about memorizing terminology — simply follow the visual demonstrations and verbal guidance.

## You are always invited to rest

Resting is never a failure. If a sequence feels like too much, return to child's pose or sit comfortably and breathe. Your instructor will be glad you are listening to your body's signals.

> There is no such thing as being "bad at yoga". There is only practicing honestly where you are today.

## After class

You might feel calm, energized or mildly sore the following morning. All of that is a natural part of [building a sustainable weekly habit](/journal/how-to-build-a-sustainable-weekly-yoga-habit/). Drink water, notice how your joints feel and consider reserving your next visit while the momentum is fresh.

## Questions?

If anything felt unfamiliar, speak with your instructor after class or [reach out to our studio team](/contact/). We are always happy to help you choose the right path forward.
