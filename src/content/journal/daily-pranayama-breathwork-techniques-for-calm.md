Breathwork is one of the most accessible practices in yoga. It requires no mat, no studio wardrobe and no free hour. You can practice at your desk, on a morning commute or in the quiet minute before an important conversation.

This guide shares three foundational techniques we teach in our [Breathwork Lab sessions](/#sessions), along with simple ways to weave them into an ordinary day.

## Why conscious breathing matters

Your breath is one of the few autonomic rhythms you can regulate directly. Slowing the cadence and gently lengthening the exhalation signals safety to the nervous system, which is why restorative practices always begin with breath awareness.

You do not need a background in physiology to notice the shift. Most practitioners feel their shoulders soften within two minutes.

## Three techniques to practice

### Extended exhale

Breathe in smoothly through the nose for a count of four, then exhale softly through the nose for a count of six. Keep the effort gentle — if the count feels strained, shorten it to three and five. Repeat for six to ten rounds.

### Box breathing

Inhale for four counts, hold softly at the top for four, exhale for four and pause at the bottom for four. Picture tracing the four equal sides of a square. This steady cadence helps anchor attention when thoughts feel scattered.

### Three-part diaphragmatic breath

Lie comfortably on your back or sit tall with one hand resting on your abdomen and the other on your ribcage. As you inhale, allow the lower belly to expand first, followed by the side ribs and finally the upper chest. Exhale smoothly in reverse order. Pair this with a [mindful morning stretch ritual](/journal/mindful-morning-movement-and-stretch-ritual/) or use it before sleep.

## Fitting breathwork into your week

The most effective technique is the one you actually remember to use:

- **Morning:** two minutes of extended exhales before checking messages.
- **Work transitions:** three unhurried breaths before opening your laptop.
- **Afternoon reset:** one round of box breathing instead of a second espresso.
- **Evening wind-down:** three-part breath in bed with the lights dimmed.

## A note on comfort

Pranayama should feel steadying, never forced. If you ever feel light-headed, return immediately to your natural breath rhythm. If you are pregnant or managing a cardiovascular or respiratory condition, consult your physician and [speak with our instructors](/#instructors) so we can tailor gentle options for you.
