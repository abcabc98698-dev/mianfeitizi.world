Most people do not stop practicing yoga because they lose interest. They stop because the schedule they set was designed for an idealized week, and idealized weeks rarely happen. A practice that endures is built around the week you actually live.

## Start smaller than you think you should

It is tempting to begin with an ambitious pledge: five studio sessions every week without fail. Two weeks later a project deadline arrives, travel comes up or energy dips, and the all-or-nothing plan unravels.

Instead, choose a baseline commitment that feels genuinely sustainable:

- **Two studio classes per week**, reserved in advance on the [weekly timetable](/#timetable).
- **Ten minutes at home** on one additional morning — a short [morning mobility ritual](/journal/mindful-morning-movement-and-stretch-ritual/) and a quiet pause.

Once that rhythm feels automatic for a month, add another session if you wish. Progress built on a steady foundation stays with you.

## Anchor your practice to an existing cue

Habits take root faster when attached to something you already do every day. A few reliable anchors our [studio members](/#member-stories) use:

1. Unrolling the mat immediately after brewing morning tea or coffee.
2. Reserving the evening class that starts right after work so there is no gap to talk yourself out of going.
3. Practicing five minutes of [extended exhale breathing](/journal/daily-pranayama-breathwork-techniques-for-calm/) right before bed.

The reliability of the cue matters far more than the time of day.

## Measure attendance, not depth of posture

Physical adaptation in yoga is real, yet it rarely unfolds in a straight line. Some days your hamstrings and hips feel open; other days everything feels tight. If you only judge progress by how deep a fold looks, it is easy to lose heart.

Track showing up instead. Marking each completed session on a calendar gives you honest feedback on your consistency — and reminds you to balance active days with [dedicated recovery and rest days](/journal/active-recovery-and-restorative-yoga-rest-days/).

> The most valuable session is the one you actually begin. Everything else is a bonus.

## Plan your minimum for busy weeks

Travel, family commitments and heavy workloads will occasionally interrupt your normal routine. Decide ahead of time what your minimum viable practice looks like:

- One restorative studio class on the weekend.
- Five minutes of supine twists and breathwork before sleep.
- A brisk walk outdoors where you pay attention to posture and stride.
