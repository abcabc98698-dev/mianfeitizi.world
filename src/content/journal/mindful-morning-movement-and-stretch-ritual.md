Many mornings begin in reactive mode: an alarm, a screen full of overnight notifications and a rush toward the door. You do not need an hour-long routine to change that trajectory — just five intentional minutes of movement and breath.

## Delay the digital scroll

Try leaving your phone out of reach for the first fifteen minutes after waking. Giving your attention a quiet buffer allows you to choose your pace for the day rather than reacting to someone else's urgency.

A simple adjustment: charge your phone outside the bedroom and wake to a dedicated bedside alarm.

## Move your spine for five minutes

A brief floor sequence wakes up stiff joints without feeling like a full workout. Move slowly through these five shapes:

1. **Child's pose** — five unhurried breaths to lengthen the lower back.
2. **Cat and cow tilts** — eight rounds, matching spinal flexion and extension to your breath.
3. **Downward-facing dog** — pedal the heels gently for five breaths to wake the calves and hamstrings.
4. **Ragdoll forward fold** — bend the knees generously and let the neck release completely.
5. **Standing overhead reach** — roll up vertebra by vertebra and inhale the arms tall.

Five minutes on a bedroom rug or mat is enough to shift how your posture feels all morning. For deeper joint preparation, explore how [functional mobility and active strength](/journal/functional-joint-mobility-and-active-strength-yoga/) work together.

## Pair movement with warmth

A mug of herbal tea, warm lemon water or your morning coffee becomes a grounding sensory anchor when enjoyed without a screen. Notice the warmth in your hands and the natural light in the room.

## Choose a single daily intention

Before stepping into your schedule, pick one clear word for the day — *steady*, *spacious*, *present*. Return to that cue alongside a round of [conscious breathwork](/journal/daily-pranayama-breathwork-techniques-for-calm/) whenever the afternoon speeds up.

> You cannot script everything that happens today, but you can always choose how you begin it.
